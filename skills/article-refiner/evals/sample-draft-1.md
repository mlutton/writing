# some thoughts on meeting notes

In the busy world of volunteer projects, it is very important to make sure
that information is shared effectively. Taking notes is one of the ways we
can do that. But I think we get a little mixed up about what the notes are
supposed to do.

I was helping with a neighborhood seed swap last spring. At the planning
meeting we talked about signs, tables, opening hours, and what to do if it
rained. I wrote down nearly all of it. Later in the week someone asked me if
we had agreed to bring the tables inside when it rained. I found three
paragraphs about tables and still couldn't tell.

We hadn't decided. That took an extra round of messages to figure out,
because the notes made every idea sound like it had about the same weight.
I could have written one line saying we still needed a rain plan. Instead
I'd recorded the conversation in the order it happened, which was only
helpful if you wanted to relive the meeting.

Now I put the decisions at the top. Underneath I put the things someone
said they'd do, along with who said it and when they expected to get to it.
Anything we're still talking about goes under open questions. If nobody
volunteered to check whether we could borrow an extra table, I leave that
as a question. Putting it in the action list just makes it look like
somebody has it covered.

I think this format is pretty common now. Studies show a lot more groups
have switched to this kind of notes lately, and they spend much less time
following up. I can't remember where I heard that. Anyway, you don't need
any special software. We used a shared document and everyone could leave a
comment if I got a decision wrong.

There are still reasons to keep a bit of the discussion. If we chose the
side entrance because the front steps were blocked, write that down next
to the decision. Otherwise we'll spend the next meeting having the same
argument when someone suggests the front entrance again.

Anyway, that's my take on meeting notes.
