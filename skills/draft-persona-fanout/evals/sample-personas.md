# Fictional reader profiles for this eval

These three profiles were created for the meeting-notes exercise.

## First-time note taker

- **Context:** This reader has agreed to take notes at the next meeting of
  a neighborhood group. They have read other people's notes and have yet
  to try a format themselves. They expect to read the article just before
  the meeting and use whatever they can remember without opening it again.
- **What they want:** An example showing where to put a decision, a task,
  and an open question. They want to know what to do when someone says
  “we should” and the meeting moves on before anyone volunteers.
- **What loses them:** Abstract advice that assumes they already know
  when a conversation has turned into a decision.

## Returning volunteer

- **Context:** This reader missed the last planning session. It is a busy
  week, and they have time to scan the notes between two errands. They
  mainly want to know what the group decided and whether anything falls
  to them before the next event.
- **What they want:** A quick, scannable way to find the result, including
  unresolved questions that might affect their task. They welcome a short
  explanation of why a choice was made if it changes what they should do.
- **What loses them:** Long examples or background between the opening
  and the practical advice, especially when they repeat the same point.

## Community bulletin editor

- **Context:** This reader puts together a short bulletin for local
  groups and occasionally links to advice that subscribers can adapt.
  Personal experience is welcome when the article makes clear that the
  example comes from one group.
- **What they want:** A distinction between what happened at the seed
  swap and what the author says happens across many groups. They would
  ask for a source before repeating a claim about time saved.
- **What loses them:** Broad references to unnamed studies or results
  that the article gives no way to check.
